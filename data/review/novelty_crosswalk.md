## Page reference convention

For **Bond, Drake and Becker 2026** the PDF page number *n* corresponds to journal page 356 + *n*. Citations below give the journal page with the PDF page in brackets, for example "j.359 [PDF p.3]".
For **Bond, Drake and Luciano 2015** the PDF page *n* corresponds to journal page 445 + *n*.

## Documented problems in the Bond 2026 early-psychosis pool

Each of these is a factual discrepancy in the published record, not a matter of taste.

1. **The published confidence interval is wrong in the Results text.** The Results give "OR: 3.39; 95% CI: 1.13-3.15" (j.359 [PDF p.3]), whereas the abstract and Fig. 1 give 2.11 to 5.46 (j.357 [PDF p.1]; j.365 [PDF p.9]). The misprinted interval is the substance-use-disorder pool's interval from the same page. The January 2026 correction does not fix it.
2. **Killackey 2019 is pooled at 6 months, not at the longest window of 24 months or less.** Bond's Table 1 records "Months of follow-up: 6" for Killackey 2019 (j.360 [PDF p.4]) and uses 47/66 versus 29/60. The trial in fact followed to 18 months and found a significant advantage only in months 0 to 6 (Killackey 2019, j.79: "no significant between-group differences in odds of employment were seen at 6-12 and 12-18 months (P = 0.288 and P = 0.594, respectively)"). The shortest and most favourable window was pooled, contrary to the review's own stated rule at j.358 [PDF p.2].
3. **Killackey 2019's fidelity was self-audited, not independently assessed**, contrary to the stated inclusion criterion. The trial states that it was not possible to engage an independent evaluator and that fidelity was assessed by self-administration of the Supported Employment Fidelity Scale (Killackey 2019, Results). Bond nonetheless rates it "Good" (j.360 [PDF p.4]).
4. **The Jäckel 2025 cell cannot be reconciled with the source.** Bond's Fig. 1 enters Jäckel as 31/39 versus 21/39, N = 78 (j.365 [PDF p.9]) and Table 1 as "IPS 79% vs Ctl 54%", n = 78 (j.361 [PDF p.5]). Jäckel's own intention-to-treat denominators are 46 and 44 (N = 90), giving 36/46 (78.3%) versus 24/44 (54.6%) for the primary composite outcome and 38/46 (82.6%) versus 26/44 (59.1%) for employment, education or training on at least one day. The percentages match but the denominators do not, so Bond's counts appear to be percentages rescaled onto equal arms of 39.
5. **A fidelity percentage does not reconcile.** "13 (76%) reporting IPS fidelity to be fair rather than good or excellent on at least one assessment" (j.359 [PDF p.3]); 13 of 19 is 68%, not 76%.
6. **A baseline-employment sensitivity analysis is asserted but not shown.** "supplementary analyses excluding participants employed at baseline did not materially change the results (analyses not shown)" (j.359 [PDF p.3]).
7. **The same trial appears with different denominators across the two Bond reviews.** Bond 2015 records the Australian trial as "Killackey 2012, 48/67 versus 28/59" (Fig. 2a, j.452 [PDF p.7]), sourced to a one-page conference abstract (reference list, j.456 [PDF p.11]); Bond 2026 records it as "Killackey 2019, 47/66 versus 29/60" (Fig. 1, j.365 [PDF p.9]). Bond 2015's fourth pooled study, Nuechterlein, was cited as "submitted for publication".

## Implications for the novelty and positioning statement

The following wording is defensible from the sources and does **not** claim to be the first meta-analysis in this area.

> Individual placement and support in early psychosis has been synthesised quantitatively before. Bond, Drake and Luciano (2015) pooled four comparative studies (odds ratio 3.66, 95% CI 1.93 to 6.93), and Bond, Drake and Becker (2026) pooled five randomised trials as one of five disability subgroups in an umbrella review of new populations (odds ratio 3.39, 95% CI 2.11 to 5.46, N = 393, I squared 11.6%). The present review is not the first meta-analysis of employment in early psychosis, and does not claim to be. It differs from those syntheses in four respects that bear directly on what the pooled estimate means.
>
> First, **estimand**. Bond 2026 pools "the number of participants competitively employed during follow-up divided by the final analytic sample size" (j.358). That construct does not distinguish current employment at an assessment, employment during a stated interval, and any employment since baseline. Our audit of 628 candidate results across 24 reports shows that this distinction is not cosmetic: of the trials Bond pools, none reports a point prevalence of employment at any follow-up assessment, and the counts Bond uses come from a 0 to 6 month window for Killackey 2019, a 6 to 12 month window for Erickson 2021, a 7 to 18 month window for Nuechterlein 2020 and a 0 to 12 month composite of employment, education or training for Jäckel 2025. We therefore separate a point-prevalence estimand from a period estimand and freeze each before selecting results.
>
> Second, **comparator**. Bond's eligibility admits "services as usual **or an alternative vocational model**" (j.358), so an IPS-versus-usual-care contrast and an IPS-versus-active-vocational-rehabilitation contrast are pooled together. We restrict the primary trial pool to IPS plus usual care versus usual care and treat an active vocational comparator as a prespecified sensitivity analysis.
>
> Third, **method**. All three prior syntheses are frequentist, use Mantel-Haenszel random effects, quantify heterogeneity with an I squared threshold, and report no prediction interval or between-study standard deviation in the text. Bond 2026 computes its estimates with a free online tool (j.358). None registers a protocol, applies a formal risk-of-bias instrument, reports a PRISMA flow, or assesses publication bias; Bond 2015 states this limitation explicitly (j.454). We register the protocol, apply a risk-of-bias instrument, and estimate a Bayesian random-effects model with an explicit heterogeneity prior and a prediction interval.
>
> Fourth, **scope**. Bond 2026 pools competitive employment rate only. It reports no pooled estimate for hours, weeks worked, earnings, job tenure, time to first job, or education for the early-psychosis group, and no pooled estimate for observational or prevalence evidence, which its randomised-trial eligibility excludes. Bond 2026 itself calls for exactly this kind of work: reviewing a large non-randomised programme evaluation, it notes that the higher rate observed there "suggests the need for further research to determine the best estimate of the employment rate for IPS clients in routine practice" (j.367). Bond 2015 likewise concludes that "the field needs longer-term and more rigorous studies of employment services within early psychosis programmes" (j.454).

Two claims to avoid. First, do not describe Bond, Drake and Campbell (2016) as a prior review of IPS in early psychosis. It is a pooled secondary analysis of 109 under-30s from four severe-mental-illness trials that explicitly excluded recent-onset clients (j.302), it performed no search and no meta-analysis, and it is not cited in Bond 2026. Second, do not assert that Bond 2026 imposed a 24-month eligibility cap; the 24-month rule is a tie-break between multiple reports of the same study (j.358).

## Decisions the authors must make

1. **Jäckel 2025 eligibility.** Jäckel is in Bond's early-psychosis pool but is not among this project's 23 extracted studies. Three sub-decisions follow.
   - *Is the outcome admissible?* Jäckel's primary outcome and every full-arm result are the composite of employment, education or training. The only employment-specific effect is an odds ratio of 3.5 (95% CI 1.07 to 11.4) in a subgroup defined as "participants who were not in education or training during the follow-up period" (Jäckel 2025, PDF p.4), which conditions on post-randomisation status and reports no arm counts. It is not a full-arm employment effect and should not be pooled as one.
   - *Which denominators?* If Jäckel is included, decide whether to use its printed 46 and 44 or Bond's 39 and 39, and say so. They cannot both be right (see discrepancy 4 above).
   - *Does adding Jäckel change the trial pool's identity?* Its comparator is standard care with referral to traditional vocational rehabilitation, which is closer to usual care than Nuechterlein's, so admitting Jäckel while excluding Nuechterlein needs an explicit rationale on the comparator dimension rather than on the outcome dimension.
2. **Nuechterlein 2020 comparator.** The exclusion from the primary pool rests on the comparator being brokered vocational rehabilitation plus social skills training, "matched in time to WFM" and equated for treatment intensity (Nuechterlein 2020, j.22). Decide and record whether the primary estimand is "IPS versus usual care" (Nuechterlein out) or "IPS versus any comparator" (Nuechterlein in). If the former, note that the intervention is also a package, IPS plus the Workplace Fundamentals Module, whose components cannot be separated, so the exclusion has two independent justifications. State the k = 4 sensitivity result whichever way the primary is defined.
3. **Whether to present the odds-ratio-scale comparison with Bond 2026.** Arguments for: it is the only way to place this project's estimate directly alongside the published 3.39 (2.11 to 5.46), and readers will make the comparison whether or not the authors do. Arguments against: the two estimands differ on outcome basis, comparator and study set, so a side-by-side odds ratio invites a like-for-like reading that is not warranted; and converting a risk ratio on an available-case denominator to an odds ratio adds a transformation that carries no substantive meaning. If the comparison is presented, it should be a supplementary analysis on Bond's own inclusion rules, labelled as a reconstruction of Bond's pool rather than as this review's result, and it must state that the Results text of Bond 2026 prints the wrong confidence interval.
4. **Ancillary decisions surfaced by the audit that bear on the crosswalk.** Whether to report the Bond 2026 discrepancies in the manuscript or only in a supplement; and whether the trial pool's outcome basis is described as "competitive employment during a stated interval" rather than "at 12 months", since none of the pooled trials supports a point prevalence.
