Result appraised: any paid employment (employed only plus employed and in school) at the six-month follow-up, 124/260 = 47.7%, journal p. 866, PDF p. 4 (Q18). The numerator is stated by the authors (“Of the 124 clients who were employed”) and equals 77 employed only + 47 both employed and in school; the stated percentages (30%, 29%, 18%) are consistent with 260. Of the 124, 81 (65%) had a competitive job. The work definition is on journal p. 864, PDF p. 2 (Q7). The reference interval of the follow-up employment status is not stated; the period classification is the lead author's adjudication, not a statement in the report.

Coverage figures verified from the report:

- Analytic cohort: 325 aged 16 to 30, nonaffective psychosis for less than two years, enrolled at one of 10 OnTrackNY sites, with at least one three-month follow-up (journal pp. 863, 864; N=325 restated p. 865).
- Total enrolment during the study period, from a separate QI report: 334 enrolled, 329 (99%) retained at three months (journal p. 866). The authors attribute the 325 versus 329 difference to the timing of de-identification.
- Table 2 (journal p. 866, PDF p. 4, rendered and inspected): in the 3 to 6 month period, 17 (5%) of 325 were discharged and 46 were censored at their last observed follow-up because of staggered enrolment; 262 entered the 6 to 9 month period (325 − 17 − 46 = 262).
- Table 3 (journal p. 867, PDF p. 5, rendered and inspected): “N at follow-up” for “3 to 6 months” is 261.
- Text: 260 clients with data at the six-month follow-up (journal p. 866).
- Descriptive completeness (reviewer calculations, not pass/fail thresholds): 260/325 = 80.0% of the analytic cohort; 260/279 = 93.2% of those not administratively censored before six months; 260/262 = 99.2% of those still enrolled at six months; 260/334 = 77.8% of the QI-reported study-period enrolment (different data extract).

Discrepancies with the figures supplied for this task:

1. Six-month N is reported three ways: 260 (text, p. 866), 261 (Table 3, p. 867) and 262 (Table 2 arithmetic, p. 866). The report does not explain the difference of one or two clients. 260 is the denominator the authors attach to the employment counts, so it is the correct extraction denominator; the gap is a coverage observation, not a correction.
2. The 325 are not all enrolees: they are enrolees with at least one three-month follow-up. The QI report gives 334 enrolled in the study period (p. 866). The “at least one three-month follow-up” qualifier is stated on journal pp. 863 (Abstract) and 864 (Methods); p. 865 gives N=325 (Results text and Table 1 title) without restating the qualifier.
3. The work definition is broader than “any paid employment, including internships”: it also names competitive or noncompetitive work and self-employment (p. 864). Only 81/124 employed clients had a competitive job.
4. Page citations supplied (result p. 866, definition p. 864) are confirmed.

## What could not be determined from the report

1. The reference interval for employment status at follow-up (current status on the form date, or any paid work in the preceding quarter). The 90-day window is stated only for admission data (Q4) and hospitalisation (Q8). The period classification therefore rests on the lead author's adjudication.
2. Whether “six-month follow-up” means six months from each client's enrolment or the second end-of-quarter report (Q3 says data are submitted “at the end of every quarter”; Q11 says “months 3, 6, 9, and 12 following enrollment”), and the permitted timing window.
3. Why the six-month N is 260 in the text, 261 in Table 3 and 262 by Table 2 arithmetic.
4. Whether internships had to be paid to count as work (Q7 lists “internship” under “any paid employment” without stating this explicitly), and any minimum hours or duration.
5. How the forms combined chart review with participant and family reports (Q4) given the statement that no self-report or collateral measures were used (Q26).
6. Characteristics of the 260 contributing clients, and whether baseline employment was among the characteristics compared between dropouts and retained clients (Q15).
7. The number of eligible people referred, assessed or declining enrolment, and the characteristics of enrolees excluded for lacking a three-month follow-up (334 enrolled versus 325 analysed, Q17).
8. Individual receipt of supported employment and education (Q26).
9. The online supplement (GAF threshold table) was not reviewed; it is not expected to bear on the extracted employment result.
