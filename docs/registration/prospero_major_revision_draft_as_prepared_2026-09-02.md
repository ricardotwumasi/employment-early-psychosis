## 4. Proposed field-by-field text

### Review title

> Employment in First-Episode Psychosis: A Systematic Review and Bayesian Meta-Analysis

### Review type

> Systematic review with narrative synthesis and meta-analysis of aggregate study-level data.

Select the closest platform category or categories available rather than entering a non-standard type if PROSPERO uses controlled options.

### Condition or domain being studied

> Employment, educational participation, vocational functioning, and vocational recovery in first-episode psychosis or early psychosis within five years of onset. The quantitative synthesis focuses on paid or competitive employment prevalence and the effect of Individual Placement and Support on competitive employment.

### Rationale for the revision

> Version 1.0 registered a broad systematic review and narrative synthesis. Quantitative employment data were sufficiently available to support a meta-analysis, but the small number of clinically comparable studies and substantial heterogeneity required explicit estimands, exact likelihoods, transparent heterogeneity priors, prediction intervals, and sensitivity analyses. The revision documents the quantitative analyses that were added after registration, preserves the original frequentist analysis, and specifies the Bayesian framework used for the updated manuscript. The chronology is reported because the original frequentist results preceded the Bayesian specification and the Bayesian results preceded this PROSPERO revision.

### Review objectives

> The review has three linked objectives. First, it will synthesise factors associated with employment and vocational functioning, the quality and sustainability of vocational engagement, and interventions intended to improve vocational recovery in first-episode psychosis. Second, it will estimate the study-level point prevalence of paid or competitive employment at the assessment closest to 12 months within a 6 to 24 month window under the study-specific care and vocational-intervention conditions of eligible samples. Third, it will estimate the effect of Individual Placement and Support added to usual care, compared with usual care alone, on competitive employment in randomised trials. Narrative and quantitative results will be interpreted with target-specific risk-of-bias and certainty assessments.

### Population

> Participants with first-episode psychosis or early psychosis within five years of psychosis onset, diagnosed using a recognised diagnostic system or validated assessment, in inpatient, outpatient, community, or early-intervention settings. Reports containing broader or mixed populations will be eligible only when an extractable early-psychosis subgroup is available. Clinical high-risk states without a psychotic disorder, drug-induced or organic psychosis, bipolar disorder with psychotic features, and chronic schizophrenia without an eligible early-psychosis subgroup will be excluded.

### Intervention or exposure

> For the broad review, eligible exposures include employment status, educational or vocational engagement, occupational functioning, and vocational interventions. For the primary intervention meta-analysis, the intervention is Individual Placement and Support added to treatment as usual or usual early-intervention care. Trials of IPS plus an additional active component, comparisons with active vocational rehabilitation, non-vocational interventions, and non-randomised comparisons will be reported separately and will not enter the primary IPS-versus-usual-care synthesis.

### Comparator

> The primary intervention comparator is treatment as usual or usual early-intervention care without Individual Placement and Support. Studies without comparators may contribute to the narrative review and to the prevalence synthesis when they report an eligible employment proportion. Active vocational comparators and within-cohort employed-versus-unemployed comparisons will be synthesised separately according to their estimands.

### Study design

> The broad systematic review includes peer-reviewed quantitative and qualitative primary studies. Reviews, meta-analyses, conference abstracts, book chapters, case reports, non-English reports, and unpublished or grey literature are excluded in accordance with version 1.0. The primary prevalence synthesis may include eligible cohorts and eligible study arms reporting a point prevalence. The primary intervention synthesis includes randomised controlled trials of IPS plus usual care versus usual care. Other controlled and non-randomised designs will be tabulated or narratively synthesised but not pooled with the primary randomised comparison.

### Search sources and dates

> MEDLINE, Embase, Global Health, and APA PsycINFO were searched on the Ovid platform. PubMed and Google Scholar were searched directly. Searches covered database inception to 30 June 2026. Reference lists of included reports and relevant reviews were examined, and forward citation searching was undertaken [confirm platform and date]. The review was restricted to peer-reviewed English-language reports; grey literature and trial registers were not searched. Complete line-by-line strategies, platforms, dates, limits, and per-source result counts will be reported in the supplement. [Replace this final sentence with exact verified details before submission.]

### Selection process

### Data collection process

> One reviewer extracted study characteristics and results into a structured form, including page or table sources for quantitative values, and a second reviewer checked the extraction. Discrepancies were resolved by discussion and, when necessary, adjudication. Numerical and label corrections are preserved in a separate, source-linked corrections table; the raw extraction file is retained unchanged. [Confirm whether study authors were contacted and describe any correspondence.]

### Main outcomes

> The first primary quantitative outcome is study-level point prevalence of paid or competitive employment at the assessment closest to 12 months within a 6 to 24 month window, using the number assessed as the denominator. The pooled quantities are the median prevalence for an exchangeable study setting and, as a secondary descriptive summary, the model-implied mean prevalence across exchangeable settings. These are not interpreted as person-weighted population prevalence.
>
> The second primary quantitative outcome is competitive employment in randomised trials of Individual Placement and Support plus usual care versus usual care, expressed as an available-case risk ratio. The exact arm-level binomial model reports an odds ratio as a sensitivity analysis. Absolute effects will be presented for pre-specified plausible control risks.
>
> The broad review also examines predictors of employment and vocational functioning, duration and quality of engagement, educational participation, associations with clinical and personal recovery, and intervention effects beyond the primary IPS comparison.

### Additional outcomes and sensitivity analyses

> Secondary and sensitivity analyses include period prevalence; a construct-relaxed point-prevalence set; reproduction of the original combined point and period prevalence set; exclusion of studies at high or weak appraisal ratings; exclusion according to verified vocational-intervention exposure; missing-as-not-employed bounds; normal-normal approximation to prevalence; leave-one-study-out analyses; alternative heterogeneity priors; a four-trial active-comparator package analysis; an exact arm-level trial model; and descriptive presentation of sparse recovery correlations, standardised mean differences, and non-comparable controlled comparisons. Exploratory meta-regressions examine design, assessment time, vocational exposure, and attrition on the original broader set, adjusted for outcome basis. These analyses will be identified as secondary or exploratory.

### Strategy for data synthesis

> The broad evidence will be synthesised narratively using the Synthesis Without Meta-analysis reporting framework, organised around predictors of vocational outcome, quality and sustainability of engagement, and vocational interventions. Study design, outcome definition, follow-up, and risk of bias will be considered when interpreting direction and magnitude.
>
> The original frequentist analysis uses random-effects models in `metafor`. Employment proportions are analysed on the logit scale with restricted maximum likelihood heterogeneity estimation and Knapp-Hartung confidence intervals, with conventional Wald intervals reported where their small-sample difference is informative. The primary intervention effect is a risk ratio for IPS plus usual care versus usual care. Heterogeneity and prediction intervals are reported where estimable. These pooling methods were not specified in PROSPERO version 1.0 and are retained as the original analysis.
>
> The amended Bayesian prevalence analysis uses a binomial-normal hierarchical model: employed counts are binomial with a normal study effect on the logit scale. The pooled logit has a Normal(0, 1.5) prior and the between-study standard deviation has a half-normal prior with scale 1, with scales 0.5 and 2 as sensitivities. The amended Bayesian trial analysis uses a normal-normal random-effects model on the log risk-ratio scale, with a Normal(0, 1) prior for the pooled log risk ratio and a half-normal prior with scale 0.5 for heterogeneity, with scales 0.25 and 1 as sensitivities. An exact arm-level binomial-logit model is secondary.
>
> Results will emphasise effect magnitude, 95% credible intervals, heterogeneity, prediction intervals, posterior probabilities, absolute-effect scenarios, and sensitivity to priors and influential studies. MCMC diagnostics, prior predictive checks, posterior predictive checks, leave-one-study-out checks, and independent release reproduction will be required. A Dienes-style interval-null and directional half-normal Bayes-factor analysis will be reported as post hoc, exploratory, and prior-sensitive because the original results preceded its specification. The alternative scale and minimally interesting effect will be publicly justified, and robustness regions will be reported.

### Risk-of-bias and quality assessment

> The original broad review used the Effective Public Health Practice Project tool for quantitative studies and the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Qualitative Research, with one assessor and checking by a second reviewer [confirm]. For the amended quantitative syntheses, the three primary randomised IPS results and the active-comparator sensitivity trial will be assessed with RoB 2 at the result level. Studies contributing to the primary prevalence synthesis will be assessed with the JBI prevalence checklist. Two reviewers will assess independently, retain item-level reasons, and adjudicate disagreements. Judgements will inform interpretation and pre-specified sensitivity analyses; they will not be treated as numerical bias-adjustment scores.

### Risk of bias due to missing results

> Risk of bias due to missing results will be assessed separately for the prevalence and intervention syntheses using ROB-ME or a fully specified equivalent framework, with two reviewers and adjudication. Statistical funnel-plot, Egger, and trim-and-fill analyses from the original prevalence analysis will be retained only as limited sensitivity analyses because such methods are problematic for proportions. Statistical assessment is not reliable for the three-trial primary intervention synthesis. The exclusion of grey literature, trial registers, and non-English reports will be considered when judging risk of missing results.

### Certainty assessment

> Certainty in the primary IPS effect will be assessed with GRADE, considering result-level risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias. Certainty in the pooled prevalence will be assessed with a transparent framework covering sampling frame and coverage, non-response and attrition, outcome measurement, risk of bias, indirectness, and between-setting inconsistency. Two reviewers will make and justify judgements, with adjudication. Certainty will be communicated wherever the corresponding results are interpreted.

### Analysis software and reproducibility

> Analyses are conducted in R using `metafor`, `brms`, `cmdstanr`, and `posterior`, with CmdStan. The raw extraction is preserved; all corrections are source-linked and programmatic; seeds, software versions, input and output hashes, fit identifiers, diagnostic status, and cache status will be recorded in a release manifest. The final analysis will be rerun without cached fits in a locked environment and independently reproduced before submission. Code and non-restricted aggregate data will be archived with an immutable release and persistent identifier.
