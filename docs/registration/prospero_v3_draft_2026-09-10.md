## 1. Condition or domain being studied

**Version 2.0 text:** employment, educational participation, vocational functioning and vocational recovery in first-episode psychosis or early psychosis within five years of onset; the quantitative synthesis focuses on paid or competitive employment prevalence and the effect of Individual Placement and Support on competitive employment.

**Proposed version 3 text:** unchanged in substance. Add: the population is defined at the level of the study sample rather than by the individual diagnostic label, as set out in the Population field.

## 2. Population (Included)

**Version 2.0 text:** participants with first-episode psychosis or early psychosis within five years of psychosis onset, diagnosed using a recognised diagnostic system or validated assessment, in inpatient, outpatient, community or early-intervention settings; reports with broader or mixed populations eligible only when an extractable early-psychosis subgroup is available.

**Proposed version 3 text:**

Inclusion. Participants in a first-episode or early-psychosis sample, defined at study level by both of the following: (i) recruitment from an early intervention in psychosis service, or from a first-episode or recent-onset psychosis cohort or trial; and (ii) duration since psychosis onset, or since first treatment for psychosis, of five years or less, stated as an eligibility criterion or reported as a maximum. Either duration measure suffices. Settings may be inpatient, outpatient, community or early-intervention.

Diagnostic boundary. Any non-organic psychotic disorder treated within such an early-psychosis sample is eligible, including schizophrenia-spectrum disorders, affective psychoses (bipolar disorder and major depression with psychotic features) and substance-induced psychosis. No minimum percentage of any diagnostic group is required, and no percentage-eligible threshold is applied.

Rationale. Early intervention in psychosis services admit all non-organic first-episode psychoses (NICE guidance in England, EPPIC in Australia, and coordinated specialty care in Canada and the United States); diagnoses are unstable in the first years after a first episode; employment outcomes in these samples are service-level outcomes; and the most closely comparable syntheses (Bond 2015; Bond, Drake and Becker 2026) use the same broader early-psychosis definition.

## 3. Population (Excluded)

**Proposed version 3 text:** samples with no psychosis requirement, for example severe mental illness cohorts (such as McGahey 2016); psychosis due to organic causes; clinical high-risk states without a psychotic disorder; and any sample whose stated duration criterion exceeds five years without an extractable subgroup within the five-year window. A sample permitting onset within eight years (Eack 2011) is not eligible for the primary syntheses and is reported in a labelled duration sensitivity analysis.

The exclusions of bipolar disorder with psychotic features and of drug-induced psychosis, registered in version 2.0, are **withdrawn from the primary eligibility rule and retained as a comparison analysis**, described in the Synthesis field.

## 4. Statement of the change to eligibility (to be entered in the revision note as well as here)

This population change was decided on 10 September 2026, after the results of the review were known, and is recorded as amendment A33 in the review's dated amendment record. The pool sizes under both rules were computed and seen before the change was made: applying the version 2.0 restriction consistently to the audited reports leaves no eligible point-prevalence study and one eligible randomised trial, whereas the broader definition is expected to yield about six prevalence studies and three trials. The change is therefore reported as post hoc throughout. It is not described as prospectively registered, and the version 2.0 restriction is retained and reported rather than replaced.

## 5. Types of study (Study design)

**Proposed version 3 text:** unchanged in structure. Clarify that the primary prevalence synthesis includes eligible cohorts and eligible trial arms that report a point prevalence of paid or competitive employment, that the primary intervention synthesis includes randomised controlled trials of Individual Placement and Support added to usual care versus usual care alone, and that trials with an active vocational comparator (Nuechterlein 2020) and non-randomised comparisons are reported separately and are not pooled with the primary randomised comparison. Studies reporting only a composite of employment, education or training (Jäckel 2025) are eligible for the narrative review and contribute no employment-only quantitative result. The exclusion of studies of participants with psychotic disorders of more than five years' duration is retained and is now expressed through the Population field.

## 6. Selection process (screening)

**Version 2.0 text:** studies will be screened independently by at least two people (or person/machine combination) with a process to resolve differences.

**Proposed version 3 text:** titles and abstracts and full texts were screened by one of two reviewers, with records divided between them by record range and decisions cross-checked. This corrects the version 2.0 statement. Because each record was screened once, no agreement statistic (kappa or percentage agreement) is available. Seventeen records that could not be opened in the screening platform were not screened; they are reported in the flow diagram and are being retrieved.

## 7. Data collection process

**Proposed version 3 text:** unchanged. Data were extracted by one person and checked by another. **Authors will not be contacted for further information**, and this applies both to the investigators of included studies and to the authors of external methodological papers used to set analysis priors. No such correspondence has taken place (amendment A38).

## 8. Study risk of bias, reporting bias and certainty assessment

Unchanged from version 2.0 (result-level RoB 2 for the randomised results, JBI prevalence checklist, ROB-ME or equivalent, GRADE and the prevalence-certainty framework). Add only that appraisal of the review's own record used EPHPP (Thomas 2004) with initial ratings by one reviewer cross-checked by the other, and that the status of the Blinding component is being confirmed.

## 9. Main outcomes

**Proposed version 3 text:** the two primary quantitative outcomes are retained, with their measurement rules made explicit (amendment A36):

- Prevalence. Study-level point prevalence of current competitive or paid employment, meaning any paid work, with composites that include students, homemakers or other productive occupation excluded. The assessment nearest 12 months within a 6 to 24 month window is used, the earlier assessment being taken when two are equally close, with the number assessed as the denominator, in the whole eligible sample or a verified eligible subgroup. Samples selected on baseline employment status are not prevalence samples. The pooled quantities remain the median prevalence for an exchangeable study setting and, as a secondary descriptive summary, the model-implied mean across exchangeable settings; neither is a person-weighted population prevalence.
- Intervention. Employment attained during the earliest post-randomisation six-month interval for which per-arm counts are reported, comparing Individual Placement and Support plus usual care with usual care alone, preserving randomised-arm comparisons, expressed as an available-case risk ratio. This replaces the version 2.0 description by nominal time point and makes the interval, not the label, the estimand.

## 10. Additional outcomes

Unchanged, with two additions: a labelled duration sensitivity analysis for samples permitting onset beyond five years, and the registered-restriction comparison analysis described below.

## 11. Strategy for data synthesis

Unchanged in models: the amended Bayesian prevalence analysis remains a binomial-normal hierarchical model with a Normal(0, 1.5) prior on the pooled logit and a half-normal(1) prior on the between-study standard deviation, with scales 0.5 and 2 as sensitivities; the amended Bayesian trial analysis remains a normal-normal random-effects model on the log risk-ratio scale with a Normal(0, 1) prior and a half-normal(0.5) heterogeneity prior, with scales 0.25 and 1 as sensitivities; an exact arm-level binomial-logit model is secondary; the original frequentist analysis is reported in full as a comparator. Added for version 3:

1. **Registered-restriction comparison analysis.** The version 2.0 population restriction is applied consistently as a labelled comparison analysis. Under that restriction no study meets the point-prevalence estimand, so no prevalence synthesis is estimable, and one randomised trial is eligible and is reported individually. This is reported whatever the primary analysis shows.
2. **Sparse-pool rule.** With no compatible studies, no estimable synthesis is reported; with one, that study is reported without a between-study model; with two, any hierarchical summary is explicitly exploratory, with individual effects, wide prior sensitivity and no transportability claim; with three or more, the approved model is fitted if the estimand is coherent, without treating the count as evidence of adequacy. Eligibility is not broadened, outcomes are not combined and endpoints are not changed in order to escape a sparse pool.
3. **Absolute effects.** Primary relative-effect inference stays on the risk-ratio scale. Absolute-risk scenarios are reported from the separately labelled arm-level binomial-logit (odds ratio) model, as treated risk logistic(logit(p0) + beta) and the difference from p0 within each posterior draw, stated as conditional on the assumed control risk and on that model's assumptions. This supersedes the version 2.0 sentence "Absolute effects will be presented for pre-specified plausible control risks", whose method was verified infeasible because multiplying risk-ratio draws by a control risk produces treated risks above one.

## 12. Revision note (amendment chronology paragraph)

Proposed text for the revision note:

Version 1.0 was published on 26 May 2026 and specified a narrative synthesis with no pooling model. A major revision was submitted on 2 September 2026 and published as version 2.0 on 5 September 2026; it added the Bayesian estimands, priors, sensitivity analyses and appraisal methods, and recorded that the original frequentist results preceded the Bayesian specification, so that the Bayesian analysis is registered by amendment and is not prospectively registered. This version 3 revision was decided on 10 September 2026, after all frequentist and Bayesian results were available and after the pool sizes under both population rules had been computed. It changes the eligible population to a broader early-psychosis definition (amendment A33), retains the version 2.0 restriction as a labelled comparison analysis that reports no eligible prevalence study and a single eligible trial, corrects the description of the screening procedure to divided single screening with cross-checking (amendment A34), states the measurement rules for both estimands (amendment A36), adds a sparse-pool rule (amendment A26) and replaces the absolute-effect method with one that cannot produce impossible risks (amendment A27). All of these changes are post hoc and are reported as such. Every change, its date, its reason and the results that were visible when it was made are recorded in the review's dated amendment record.
